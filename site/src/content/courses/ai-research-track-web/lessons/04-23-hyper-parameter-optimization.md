It is the right default baseline.</li>\n<li><strong>Bayesian optimisation</strong> (Gaussian process, TPE in Optuna) uses past trials to pick the next. It helps most when each trial is expensive and there are few dimensions.</li>\n<li><strong>Early stopping of bad trials</strong> (Hyperband, ASHA, Optuna&#39;s pruners) often saves more compute than a clever sampler.</li>\n</ul>\n<h3>Search on the right scale</h3>\n<p>Learning rate and weight decay vary over orders of magnitude, so sample them <strong>log-uniformly</strong>:</p>\n<pre class=\"code\"><code class=\"language-python\">import optuna\ndef objective(trial):\n    lr = trial.suggest_float(&quot;lr&quot;, 1e-5, 1e-2, log=True)\n    width = trial.suggest_int(&quot;width&quot;, 32, 512, log=True)\n    return train_and_validate(lr, width)        # return validation loss\nstudy = optuna.create_study(direction=&quot;minimize&quot;)\nstudy.optimize(objective, n_trials=40)\n</code></pre>\n<h3>The trap</h3>\n<p>Picking the best of 40 trials on the validation set overfits the validation set. Report final numbers on a <strong>held-out test set</strong> touched once, and when comparing two methods, give both the same tuning budget. Unequal tuning is one of the most common reasons a published &quot;improvement&quot; does not reproduce (lesson 09.01 and Melis et al., 2017).</p>\n<blockquote class=\"callout\"><p><strong class=\"callout-label\">Key idea</strong> Use random search as the baseline, log scales for rates, and early stopping for bad trials. Tune on validation, report on test, and give every method you compare the same budget.</p>\n</blockquote>\n</section></details>"
