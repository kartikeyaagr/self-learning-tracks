answer: "Attention weights show where one layer&#39;s head read from, not how much that information affected the output; values pass through many layers, residual connections and MLPs afterwards. Different attention patterns can give the same prediction (Jain and Wallace, 2019). Use attribution or ablation methods for causal claims."

Once attention clicks, the rest of the Transformer is ordinary layers you already know.</p>\n<h3>The problem it solves</h3>\n<p>Read: &quot;The <strong>animal</strong> didn&#39;t cross the street because <strong>it</strong> was too tired.&quot;</p>\n<p>To understand &quot;it&quot;, you need to look back at &quot;animal&quot;, not &quot;street&quot;. The word that matters can be anywhere in the sentence. A layer that mixes words in fixed positions cannot do this. Attention lets each word <strong>choose which other words to read from</strong>, based on their content.</p>\n<h3>The idea in plain words: a soft lookup</h3>\n<p>Think of a Python dictionary. You give a <strong>query</strong>, it finds the <strong>key</strong> that matches exactly, and returns that key&#39;s <strong>value</strong>.</p>\n<p>Attention is a <em>soft</em> version:</p>\n<ol>\n<li>Compare the query with <strong>every</strong> key, giving a similarity score (a dot product, lesson 02.01).</li>\n<li>Turn the scores into weights that add up to 1 (softmax,
