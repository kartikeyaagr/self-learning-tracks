Risky: the space of ideas is large and most do not work.</li>\n<li><strong>Goal-driven.</strong> Start from a capability or question you care about and work backwards. Schulman prefers this, because a clear goal tells you which results matter and lets you change methods freely.</li>\n</ul>\n<p>He also recommends keeping a notebook of ideas and a daily log of what you tried and what happened, and working on problems where you have some unusual advantage (a skill, a dataset, an angle others lack).</p>\n<h3>Scoping for Colab</h3>\n<p>Your constraints are a free T4 or similar and limited hours. Good project shapes under those constraints:</p>\n<ul>\n<li><strong>Reproduction with a twist.</strong> Reproduce a small result (a GRPO run on GSM8K with a 0.5B model, a scaling curve on TinyStories), then test one change the paper did not. The ML Reproducibility Challenge is a recognised venue for exactly this.</li>\n<li><strong>Analysis of an existing phenomenon.</strong> Measure something about a released model (how GRPO changes response length, where a LoRA update concentrates its rank) with careful controls.</li>\n<li><strong>A careful negative result.</strong> Showing that a claimed improvement disappears under proper seeds and intervals is publishable when done well.</li>\n</ul>\n<p>Goodfellow et al. §11.1 add a step that most beginners skip: <strong>decide the metric and the target before you start.</strong> What number would make this project a success, and how would you know if it is impossible? Keshav, How to Read a Paper · Andrew Ng, CS230 lecture on reading research papers</p><p>Keshav&#39;s <strong>three-pass method</strong> keeps you from spending three hours on a paper that deserved ten minutes.</p>\n<ol>\n<li><strong>First pass (5-10 minutes).</strong> Title, abstract, introduction, section headings, conclusion, glance at the maths and the references you recognise. Mark unread references worth following.</li>\n<li><strong>Third pass (several hours).</strong> Virtually re-implement the paper: make the same assumptions and recreate the work, then compare.
