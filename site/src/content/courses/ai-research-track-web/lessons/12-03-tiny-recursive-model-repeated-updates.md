The <strong>Tiny Recursive Model</strong> (TRM, October 2025) does something different: a single 2-layer network, about 7M parameters, is applied over and over to its own outputs, refining an answer in a hidden space. Trained only on each benchmark&#39;s small training set, it scored 45% on ARC-AGI-1 and 8% on ARC-AGI-2, above several large reasoning LLMs reported in the paper (DeepSeek R1, o3-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro), with under 0.01% of their parameters.</p>\n<h3>The loop</h3>\n<p>TRM keeps three things: the embedded question <span class=\"katex\"><span class=\"katex-html\" aria-hidden=\"true\"><span class=\"katex-base\"><span class=\"katex-strut\" style=\"height:0.4306em;\"></span><span class=\"mord mathnormal\">x</span></span></span></span>, the current answer <span class=\"katex\"><span class=\"katex-html\" aria-hidden=\"true\"><span class=\"katex-base\"><span class=\"katex-strut\" style=\"height:0.625em;vertical-align:-0.1944em;\"></span><span class=\"mord mathnormal\" style=\"margin-right:0.0359em;\">y</span></span></span></span>, and a latent &quot;scratchpad&quot; <span class=\"katex\"><span class=\"katex-html\" aria-hidden=\"true\"><span class=\"katex-base\"><span class=\"katex-strut\" style=\"height:0.4306em;\"></span><span class=\"mord mathnormal\" style=\"margin-right:0.044em;\">z</span></span></span></span>. One step of reasoning is:</p>\n<pre class=\"code\"><code class=\"language-python\">def recursion(x, y, z, n):\n    for _ in range(n):           # think: update the scratchpad several times\n        z = net(x + y + z)\n    y = net(y + z)               # act: update the answer from the scratchpad\n    return y, z\n</code></pre>\n<p>The same tiny <code>net</code> does both jobs. Several recursions are chained, and the whole thing is repeated across up to 16 <strong>deep-supervision</strong> steps: after each, the answer is decoded and given a loss, and <span class=\"katex\"><span class=\"katex-html\" aria-hidden=\"true\"><span class=\"katex-base\"><span class=\"katex-strut\" style=\"height:1em;vertical-align:-0.25em;\"></span><span class=\"mopen\">(</span><span class=\"mord mathnormal\" style=\"margin-right:0.0359em;\">y</span><span class=\"mpunct\">,</span><span class=\"mspace\" style=\"margin-right:0.1667em;\"></span><span class=\"mord mathnormal\" style=\"margin-right:0.044em;\">z</span><span class=\"mclose\">)</span></span></span></span> are carried into the next step with gradients cut. A small learned halting head decides when the answer is good enough to stop early.</p>\n<h3>What it simplified from HRM</h3>\n<p>The Hierarchical Reasoning Model (HRM, 27M parameters) used two networks running at different speeds, inspired by brain rhythms, and justified a one-step gradient approximation with a fixed-point argument. TRM drops the biology and the fixed-point theory: one network, and backpropagation through the full final recursion. It is smaller and generalises better.</p>\n<h3>Why depth through repetition works here</h3>\n<ul>\n<li><strong>Effective depth without parameters.</strong> Applying 2 layers dozens of times gives a very deep computation from very few weights, which limits overfitting on about a thousand training puzzles.</li>\n<li><strong>Iterative refinement.</strong> Puzzles like Sudoku and ARC grids are solved by proposing an answer and correcting it, which is what the loop does.</li>\n<li><strong>Small data.</strong> The paper found that making the network larger (more layers) <em>hurt</em> generalisation in this low-data regime; more recursion helped.</li>\n</ul>\n<h3>What to be careful about</h3>\n<p>TRM is trained per task family with heavy augmentation, and it does not use language. Its scores are not comparable one-to-one with a general LLM answering zero-shot. The result is still important: for structured reasoning, a small network iterating on a latent state can rival scale.</p>\n<blockquote class=\"callout\"><p><strong class=\"callout-label\">Key idea</strong> TRM reuses one 2-layer network many times, updating a latent scratchpad and then the answer, with a loss after each of up to 16 supervision steps. Repetition gives depth without parameters, which is what generalises on tiny datasets.</p>\n</blockquote>\n</section></details>"
